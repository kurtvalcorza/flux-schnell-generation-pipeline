#### Description

`PixArt-alpha/PixArt-Sigma-XL-2-512-MS` at revision `76fb7eb5a9314bc1e4e479d2f13447517fca9be4` is the 512 × 512 member of PixArt-Σ (Chen et al., 2024), a Diffusion Transformer for text-to-image generation trained "weak-to-strong" from PixArt-α on higher-quality captioned images: 28 transformer blocks of hidden size 1,152 with 16 attention heads, adaptive layer-norm conditioning on the timestep (`ada_norm_single`), self-attention over 2 × 2 patches of a 64 × 64 × 4 latent and cross-attention to 300 caption tokens embedded by a frozen T5-XXL encoder (T5 v1.1 architecture, 24 layers, d_model 4,096, 4.76 B parameters); the latent is the SDXL VAE's 8× compression of the image, and the transformer predicts the noise and a learned variance (8 output channels) under a 1,000-step linear-β schedule. The packaged file, `transformer/diffusion_pytorch_model.safetensors` (2,443,492,488 bytes, SHA-256 `20f3328e…`), holds 603 tensors and 610,856,096 parameters in float32. Inference encodes the prompt once, runs classifier-free guidance (an empty negative prompt) through a DPM-Solver++ sampler — 20 steps at guidance 4.5 by default — and decodes the latent with the VAE in float32.

What this repository adds is the `PixArtSigmaPipeline` class in `src/pixart_sigma_generation_pipeline/pipeline.py`: manifest verification of the three Hub snapshots before any model library is imported (`verify_snapshot`, `verify_base_snapshot`, `verify_scorer_snapshot`), fresh-clone staging at the pinned revisions (`stage_missing_files` and its two siblings), transformer construction from the installed `diffusers` package with a rank-8 LoRA injected by `peft` on the query, key, value and output projections of both attentions of every block (`build_transformer`, 448 tensors, 4,128,768 parameters — LoRA B is zero at initialisation, so the untrained adapter *is* the frozen model), lazy loading of the T5-XXL encoder with a prompt cache that outlives it (`encode_prompts`, `release_text_encoder`, `export_prompt_cache`, `import_prompt_cache`), seeded generation through the upstream `PixArtSigmaPipeline` with `text_encoder=None` (`generate`), a held-out denoising-MSE evaluation at five fixed timesteps with seeded noise so that the frozen and adapted models are scored on identical inputs (`evaluate`), bounded LoRA fine-tuning on the noise-prediction objective with validation-loss epoch selection (`adapt`), and a safetensors adapter artifact that is digest-verified and scope-checked before deserialization (`save_artifact`, `from_artifact`). `samples.py` pins 60 CC0 iNaturalist photographs by byte size and SHA-256, generates one template caption per species, draws a seeded stratified split and reads/writes the `captions.csv` + images layout for BYOD; `metrics.py` holds the frozen CLIP scorer and the three CLIP measures with their real-photo ceiling.

Two things about this row are stated rather than hidden. **The text encoder is a 19 GB float32 download that runs in float16 and is dropped before training**, because it does not fit beside a training graph on a 16 GB GPU; every prompt the pipeline will use should be encoded first — `generate`, `evaluate` and `adapt` encode a missing prompt on demand, which reloads the released encoder and costs the memory and the minute it takes. **Generation has no ground truth:** the only assertion with a direction is the one the procedure guarantees — the kept epoch's validation denoising loss is no higher than the frozen model's — while the test loss, the CLIP scores and the real-photo ceiling are read as observations, and no human evaluation, FID or preference study exists here.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

###### Primary Intended Uses

Two tasks are exposed. **Text-to-image generation:** input is one or more prompts of 1..1,000 characters (truncated to 300 T5 tokens, reported), a seed, a step count (1..100) and a guidance scale (1..20); output is one 512 × 512 RGB image per prompt (image *i* seeded with `seed + i`), the pixel mean and the generation record. **Adaptation to captioned images:** given at least four `{id, image, caption}` records (shorter side 256..4,096 px, centre-cropped to 512²), the pipeline fine-tunes the LoRA on the noise-prediction objective with a small learning rate and validation-loss epoch selection, generates with the adapted transformer and exports the 448 LoRA tensors as an adapter of about 17 MB. Envisioned application domains are teaching and evaluating diffusion-model fine-tuning on a small captioned set — a species, a product, a house style — in research or the DIMER model workbench; prototyping a generation service whose adaptation is then measured on the operator's own held-out images; and producing illustrative images for exploratory work. In a larger system the pipeline is a single-prompt generator and an adaptation baseline; it is not a content-moderated image service, a photo-realistic reference generator, or an image editor.

###### Primary Intended Users

Intended users are machine-learning engineers and researchers who work with latent diffusion models, and educators demonstrating parameter-efficient fine-tuning, in research, teaching or the DIMER model workbench. The pipeline assumes its users understand what a LoRA changes and what it does not (the attention projections of the transformer; never the VAE, the encoder or the rest of the weights), that a training loss is not an image-quality score and a CLIP similarity is not a human judgement, that six template captions make the sample a teaching example rather than a dataset, and that the Open RAIL++-M licence's use-based restrictions bind every image and adapter they produce.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** the pipeline generates 512 × 512 images from text and fine-tunes a LoRA on the transformer. It does not run the 1024-MS or 2K-MS checkpoints, PixArt-α, ControlNet or image-to-image conditioning, DreamBooth identifiers, full fine-tuning, safety filtering of prompts or images, aspect-ratio binning, or any human-preference or FID/KID evaluation; and it does not reproduce the published quality results.
2. **Input boundary:** records are RGB images (PIL or file path) with sides within 256..4,096 px and captions of 1..1,000 characters; datasets hold 4..2,000 records with unique ids; prompts are non-empty strings. Other sizes, non-image files, empty captions or duplicate ids are not inputs. Validation is structural: nothing checks that a caption describes its image or that a prompt is one the model can render.
3. **Decision boundary:** not for producing images presented as photographs of real events, people or products, for depicting identifiable people, for any use the Open RAIL++-M restrictions exclude, or for any downstream decision that treats a generated image as evidence. A generated image from this pipeline is an experiment output.
4. **Provenance boundary:** not for settings that require the encoder to stay resident (the contract releases it), that need the float32 encoder shards re-exported in another precision (this repository produces no converted file), or that forbid the RAIL licence family; the licence and size gates are documented under *DIMER deployment notes* and in `docs/WEIGHTS.md`.

#### Factors

###### Groups

The model generates images of whatever the prompt names, so the relevant groups are the subjects that prompts can depict: people of any age, gender, skin type, body, dress and culture, professions, places, and the animals and objects of the sample. Upstream trained on a large curated image corpus with re-captioned descriptions (the paper describes the data, not its demographic composition), and documents no per-group evaluation; this repository's sample prompts name six bird species and no people, and the pipeline measures nothing about how any group is rendered. A deployment that generates depictions of people must measure representation and stereotyping for its prompts itself; the people depicted, and the communities whose likeness the model reproduces or distorts, bear the harm.

###### Instrumentation

The instruments are the training photographs' cameras and, upstream, the captioning model that wrote the training descriptions; here, the iNaturalist observers' cameras (phone and DSLR, resized by iNaturalist to the "medium" 500 px variant) and one caption template per species. Photograph resolution, compression and framing reach the model through the VAE as latent detail, and the centre crop discards the edges of every non-square image (reported per record). The measuring instrument for the CLIP scores is `laion/CLIP-ViT-B-32-laion2B-s34B-b79K`, a 224 px image–text model with its own training biases: it rewards images that look like its training distribution and captions phrased like its alt-text, so a rise in a CLIP score is a rise on that instrument, not a rise in quality.

###### Environment

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `diffusers==0.40.0`, `transformers==5.17.0`, `peft==0.21.0`, `torchao==0.18.0`, `accelerate==1.15.0`, `tokenizers==0.23.2`, `sentencepiece==0.2.2`, `protobuf==7.36.2`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `huggingface-hub==1.32.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0` (`pyproject.toml`; `torchaudio` and `torchao` are never called — they are pinned because `transformers` imports the former when present and `peft`'s LoRA dispatcher refuses an old copy of the latter, both preinstalled on hosted images). The transformer runs in float16 on CUDA with the LoRA parameters in float32 under autocast and loss scaling; the VAE stays in float32 (its config forces the upcast); the text encoder runs in float16 on CUDA. A GPU of at least 15 GB is required for the encoder, and about 25 GB of disk for the three snapshots; CPU execution is not supported by the tutorial (the encoder would need 19 GB of RAM and a 512² image takes minutes). The recorded run is under *Runtime*. Data environment: the model assumes prompts in English resembling captions and renders best the subjects, styles and compositions common in its training corpus; it degrades, without any error, on precise counts, text rendering, spatial relations, rare subjects and long compositional prompts.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

The pipeline reports three kinds of number and names each. **Held-out denoising MSE** (`evaluate`): every photograph is VAE-encoded (seeded posterior sample), noised at timesteps 100 / 300 / 500 / 700 / 900 with seeded noise, and the transformer's noise prediction is scored by mean-squared error against that noise — reported as the mean over records and timesteps, per timestep and per record. The seed fixes latents, noise and timesteps, so the frozen and adapted models are compared on identical inputs; this is the training objective on held-out data, not a quality score. **CLIP scores of generated images** (`metrics.score_generations`): `clip_prompt_similarity` (mean cosine between each image and its prompt × 100), `label_accuracy` (the fraction of images whose nearest prompt among the distinct prompts is their own — zero-shot classification with an argmax rule), and `reference_similarity` (mean cosine to the mean embedding of the held-out real photographs with the same caption × 100). **The real-photo ceiling** (`real_photo_baseline`): the same three scores on the held-out photographs themselves, so a reader can see how far a generation sits from a real photograph on this instrument. Every number in this repository is sample-sanity evidence from one seeded run on 12 held-out photographs, 12 validation photographs (epoch selection only) and 12 generated images, with no dispersion estimate; no published PixArt-Σ result (FID, human preference, GenEval) is reproduced or claimed.

###### Decision thresholds

The generator has no decision rule; the only rule in the repository is the CLIP `label_accuracy` argmax (a generated image is "correct" when its nearest caption among the dataset's captions is its own), which has no threshold and is labelled as the zero-shot instrument it is. The limits fixed during development are input-contract limits, not decision thresholds — the image-side range, the caption length, the 300-token truncation, the 4..2,000 record range, the step and guidance ranges — chosen so that a rejection names a real defect and an acceptance never means "the caption is right" or "the prompt is renderable". An operator who needs a threshold (a minimum CLIP similarity before an image is kept, a maximum loss before an adaptation is accepted) must set it from their own review, and own it. `adapt` selects the epoch with the lowest validation denoising MSE, which is the only selection this repository performs.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

Reported metrics come from one seeded run — 12 held-out test photographs for the numbers, 12 validation photographs for epoch selection, two generations per test caption at fixed seeds — with no cross-validation, bootstrap, ensemble, repeated seeds or dispersion: with six captions and twelve images, any interval would be dominated by which photographs and seeds were drawn. A caller who needs one evaluates many images and seeds across many prompts and reports distributions, and owns that design. Sources of run-to-run variability: the split, the VAE posterior samples, the evaluation noise, the training noise and timesteps and the generation latents are all seeded (`torch.Generator` on the CPU); float16 autocast, non-deterministic GPU attention kernels and the gradient scaler are not controlled, so a re-run can differ in the last decimals of the adapted numbers and in individual pixels of a generated image, which is why the reload-parity check compares a seeded generation by mean absolute pixel difference rather than byte equality. CLIP similarities are cosine scores on one instrument and are **not calibrated probabilities**; the denoising loss is an objective value with no uncertainty estimate beyond the per-record spread the report lists.

#### Ethical considerations and biases

###### Data

Upstream describes PixArt-Σ's training data as a curated corpus of about 33 M high-quality images with dense re-written captions (the paper gives the sources and the captioning model), on top of PixArt-α's training; neither the images nor any per-group statistics are released, and the corpus may include depictions of people and copyrighted works whose status this repository cannot assess. This repository distributes code, tests and documentation; it does **not** vendor any weight file, any photograph or any generated image in Git. The tutorial fetches 60 research-grade iNaturalist photographs (5,789,324 bytes) from the public open-data bucket — every one CC0 1.0, observer and observation page recorded in `samples.py` — and caches them under `weights/inat-birds/`, git-ignored; no person appears in them. The operator's obligation for the images they supply at adaptation — likeness, copyright, licensing, consent — and for the images they generate is theirs; the pipeline performs no audit of either.

###### Human Life

This pipeline is not intended for decisions in health, safety, criminal justice, employment, credit, or housing, and it has not been validated or certified for any of them by anyone. No experimental validation, no regulatory clearance and no external review has taken place; the evidence in this repository is the offline unit suite, the model-backed GPU smoke and one executed run of the generated notebook on 60 real photographs, recorded under *Runtime* and in `docs/release-verification.md`. The foreseeable harms of an image generator are not in those domains but adjacent to them — fabricated depictions of real people or events, sexual or violent content, and reinforcement of stereotypes — and the repository implements no prompt or image filter; the Open RAIL++-M restrictions and the operator's own review are the only controls.

###### Mitigations

1. **Supply-chain integrity:** `MODEL_ID`, `BASE_ID`, `SCORER_ID` and their 40-hex revisions are module constants; each `verify_*_snapshot()` checks `modelId`, `revision`, and the byte size and SHA-256 of every manifest entry (3 + 12 + 9 files) before any load and raises on the first mismatch (tests flip bytes, change the identity and delete a file and assert each raises). Each `stage_missing_*()` fetches only manifest-listed files absent on disk, only at the pinned revision, only when `allow_download=True`, and refuses a manifest naming another model. `.gitattributes` carries `weights/** -text`.
2. **Safetensors only, no remote code:** every weight file in the three snapshots is safetensors; nothing is unpickled anywhere in the pipeline, `trust_remote_code` is never set, and the classes come from `diffusers`, `transformers` and `peft` on PyPI at exact pins. The transformer is loaded from the verified directory and its tensor and parameter counts (603 / 610,856,096) and the LoRA tensor count (448) are asserted.
3. **Encoder lifecycle:** the text encoder is loaded only by `encode_prompts`, in float16 on CUDA, and released by `release_text_encoder`; `generate`, `evaluate` and `adapt` encode only the prompts missing from the cache (reloading the encoder when it was released) and refuse to run on an unencoded prompt, and `import_prompt_cache` checks the shape of every entry (300 × 4,096 embeddings and a 300-element mask) before adopting it.
4. **Data integrity:** each of the 60 photographs is pinned by byte size and SHA-256 and refused on a mismatch before decoding; `dataset_manifest` records a digest over decoded pixels and captions; `check_split_disjoint` refuses an image present in two splits; the BYOD loader reads only the files `captions.csv` names.
5. **Artifact integrity and scope:** the adapter is safetensors, not a pickle. `load_artifact` validates the manifest **before** deserialising anything: the artifact format and `format_version`, the recorded base model id and revision, the components id and revision, exactly one weights entry named `adapter.safetensors` that resolves inside the artifact directory, the LoRA rank / alpha / targets, and a tensor list equal to the exact set of 448 LoRA tensors of this model; the safetensors keys must then equal that set and every shape must match. The weights file's SHA-256 is checked against the adjacent manifest — it detects corruption or drift of the file relative to that manifest, not authenticity: an actor who can replace both files can make them agree.
6. **Input integrity:** `evaluate`, `adapt`, `validate_inputs` and `validate_dataset` route through one checker, so their acceptance criteria cannot diverge; it rejects missing keys, unreadable images, sides outside 256..4,096 px, empty or over-long captions, duplicate ids and datasets outside 4..2,000 records; `generate` validates prompts, steps and guidance the same way; the centre crop and the token truncation are reported, never silent.
7. **Training discipline:** only the 448 LoRA tensors receive gradients (the base transformer, the VAE and the encoder store no graph), the learning rate defaults to 10⁻⁴ and is bounded at 10⁻², gradients are clipped at norm 1, timesteps are drawn uniformly and noise is seeded, the epoch is selected by validation loss with the frozen model as epoch 0, and `adapt` is transactional — any failure restores the LoRA to its initial values, re-freezes the model and clears the adapter.
8. **Import boundary:** manifest verification and input validation run before `torch`, `diffusers`, `transformers`, `peft`, `safetensors` or `huggingface_hub` are imported (fleet RTM-001); `tests/test_import_boundary.py` fails if a rejected request reaches a model library, and an AST check fails if any of them is imported at module level.
9. **Honest scope:** validation is labelled structural, the CLIP scores are labelled as instrument readings against a real-photo ceiling, the denoising loss is labelled as the objective, the only assertion with a direction is the guaranteed one (the kept epoch's validation loss is no higher than the frozen model's; the test loss is printed, not asserted), and no upstream quality result is claimed.

Not implemented, and therefore not claimed: no prompt or image safety filter, no watermarking of generated images, no aspect-ratio binning or resolutions other than 512², no full fine-tuning, no human or FID evaluation, no calibration of any score, and no measurement beyond the 60-photograph sample.

###### Risks and harms

1. **Fabrication:** a generated image can depict a real person, a real place or a plausible event that never happened, with photographic realism at 512²; the repository adds no watermark, no provenance metadata and no filter, so misuse is bounded only by the licence and the operator.
2. **Sexual, violent and demeaning content:** the model can render content the Open RAIL++-M licence forbids and the pipeline does not refuse it; the sample prompts are birds, which proves nothing about other prompts.
3. **Stereotypes and erasure:** the training corpus and its machine-written captions carry the biases of their sources; prompts naming professions, nationalities or bodies can be rendered stereotypically, and rare subjects can be rendered as more common ones. Nothing here measures it.
4. **Copyright and likeness:** the training data may include copyrighted works and identifiable people; an operator fine-tuning on photographs they do not own, or generating a living artist's style, carries that exposure.
5. **Overfitting on a few images:** a LoRA trained on 36 photographs of six species learns those photographs; the validation loss guards only against the worst case on the validation images, and the adapted model can lose generality on prompts far from the training captions.
6. **Metric misreading:** a lower denoising loss and a higher CLIP score are easy to read as "better images"; neither is, and the card, the README and the notebook say so at every occurrence.
7. **Memory failure modes:** the encoder, the transformer and a training graph together exceed a 16 GB GPU; a caller who keeps the encoder resident and then trains gets an out-of-memory error at the first backward pass, not earlier.
8. **Automation bias and proprietary data:** a convincing image invites use without review, and the realistic confidentiality failure is licensed stock or client imagery uploaded to a cloud notebook — inference is local, but the runtime may not be the operator's.

###### Use cases

The model must not be used to generate images that depict identifiable people without their consent, that present a generated image as a photograph of a real event, that are sexual, violent, harassing or demeaning, or that otherwise fall under the use-based restrictions of the CreativeML Open RAIL++-M licence; it must not be used as evidence about PixArt-Σ's published quality, which this repository does not measure; it must not be used to redistribute the weights or an adapter without the licence and its restrictions attached; it must not be used to fine-tune on images the operator has no right to use; and it must not be used in any way that breaches the upstream licences or the DIMER deployment terms.

## Input/output contract

- `PixArtSigmaPipeline.from_pretrained(device=None, weights_dir=None, base_dir=None, allow_download=False, use_lora=False)`: stages (when allowed) and verifies the transformer and components snapshots, loads the transformer (float16 on CUDA, with a rank-8 LoRA injected when `use_lora=True`), the VAE (float32), the tokenizer and the scheduler config. The text encoder is not loaded here.
- `encode_prompts(prompts, *, text_encoder_device=None) -> dict`: loads the T5-XXL encoder on first use (float16 on CUDA, float32 on CPU), encodes every distinct prompt plus the empty negative prompt into the cache (300 × 4,096 embeddings and the attention mask), reports counts, timings and truncations. `release_text_encoder() -> bool`, `export_prompt_cache() -> dict`, `import_prompt_cache(cache) -> int`.
- `generate(prompts, *, seed=0, steps=20, guidance_scale=4.5) -> dict` with `model` (id, revision, key, `adapted`), `steps`, `guidance_scale`, `size`, `scheduler`, `precision`, `images` (per prompt: `prompt`, `seed`, `image` (PIL, 512 × 512 RGB), `pixel_mean`), `seconds`.
- `evaluate(records, *, seed=0, batch_size=4) -> dict`: `metric`, `n_records`, `timesteps`, `seed`, `denoising_mse`, `by_timestep`, `per_record`, `adapted`, `seconds`.
- `adapt(train, val=None, *, epochs=4, lr=1e-4, batch_size=1, seed=0, progress=None) -> dict`: bounded AdamW LoRA fine-tuning (needs `use_lora=True`); returns the LoRA configuration, `trainable_names`, `n_trainable`, `n_total`, hyperparameters, `best_epoch`, per-epoch `history` (epoch 0 = the frozen model, each with `train_loss` and `val_loss`), `n_steps` and `seconds`.
- `save_artifact(output_dir, metadata=None) -> Path` writes `adapter.safetensors` (the 448 LoRA tensors) + `manifest.json` (format, base model id/revision/key, components id/revision, adaptation record, history, tensor names, file size and SHA-256); `from_artifact(...)` / `load_artifact(...)` verify all of that before deserialising.
- `validate_inputs(record) -> dict` (`id`, `size`, `resized_to`, `centre_crop`, `caption_chars`) and `validate_dataset(records, *, min_records=4, max_records=2000) -> dict` (normalised `records`, `n_records`, `n_captions`, `shorter_side`, `centre_cropped`, `resolution`, `digest`, `model_id`) raise exactly what the execution path would raise; `validate_prompts(prompts) -> list[str]`; `preprocess_image(image)` performs the resize and centre crop. `fetch_sample_dataset(cache_dir=None)` / `fetch_corpus` / `read_corpus` / `build_sample_dataset` / `split_dataset` / `load_byod_dataset(path)` / `write_dataset_csv` / `dataset_manifest` / `sample_prompts` / `check_split_disjoint` / `caption_for` are the data helpers; `metrics.ClipScorer(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False)`, `score_generations(scorer, generated, references=None)` and `real_photo_baseline(scorer, records)` are the scoring helpers.

The transformer and the adapter are unaffected either way.

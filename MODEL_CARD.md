#### Description

`black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell` at revision `741f7c3ce8b383c54771c7003378a50191e9efe9` is the Apache-2.0 member of the FLUX.1 family (Black Forest Labs, 2024): a 12 B-parameter rectified-flow transformer distilled with latent adversarial diffusion distillation so that it renders an image in one to four sampling steps without classifier-free guidance (`guidance_embeds: false`). Two frozen text encoders condition it — CLIP-L (123 M parameters), whose pooled 768-vector modulates the blocks, and T5-XXL (4.76 B, T5 v1.1 architecture), whose 256 token embeddings of size 4,096 form the text stream — and 19 double-stream blocks (image and text tokens with separate weights, joint attention) followed by 38 single-stream blocks, hidden size 3,072 with 24 heads, predict the flow velocity of a 16-channel latent (the FLUX VAE's 8× compression of the image, 64 × 64 at 512², packed 2 × 2 into 1,024 tokens of 64 channels) with rotary position ids. The snapshot in the diffusers layout holds the transformer in three bfloat16 shards (23,782,494,032 bytes; 1,156 tensors, 11,891,178,560 parameters), the T5 encoder in two (9,524,648,584 bytes), CLIP-L (246,144,352 bytes), the VAE (167,666,902 bytes), both tokenizers and the `FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler` config (`shift 1.0`, no dynamic shifting) — 23 files, 33,725,923,002 bytes.

What this repository adds is the `FluxSchnellPipeline` class in `src/flux_schnell_generation_pipeline/pipeline.py`: manifest verification of the snapshot and the scorer before any model library is imported (`verify_snapshot`, which also checks that the manifest names the pinned staging mirror, `verify_scorer_snapshot`), fresh-clone staging of the missing safetensors from `unsloth/FLUX.1-schnell` at its pinned revision (`stage_missing_files`, `stage_missing_scorer_files`), 4-bit transformer construction from the installed `diffusers` and `bitsandbytes` packages — NF4 with double quantisation, float16 compute, the parameter count asserted against the checkpoint — with a rank-8 LoRA injected by `peft` on the query, key, value and output projections of the image stream of every block (`build_transformer`, 190 modules, 380 tensors, 9,338,880 parameters; LoRA B is zero at initialisation, so the untrained adapter *is* the frozen model), a residency rule that keeps the 16-bit text encoders and the 4-bit transformer off the GPU at the same time (`encode_prompts`, `release_text_encoder`, `load_transformer`, `release_transformer`, `export_prompt_cache`, `import_prompt_cache`), seeded four-step generation through the upstream `FluxPipeline` with both encoders set to `None` and the latents decoded here through the float32 VAE (`generate`), a held-out flow-matching evaluation at five fixed noise levels with seeded noise so that the frozen and adapted models are scored on identical inputs (`evaluate`), bounded QLoRA fine-tuning on the rectified-flow velocity objective with gradient checkpointing and validation-loss epoch selection (`adapt`), and a safetensors adapter artifact that records the base identity, the staging source and the quantisation and is digest-verified and scope-checked before deserialisation (`save_artifact`, `from_artifact`). `samples.py` pins 60 CC0 iNaturalist photographs by byte size and SHA-256, generates one template caption per species, draws a seeded stratified split and reads/writes the `captions.csv` + images layout for BYOD; `metrics.py` holds the frozen CLIP scorer and the three CLIP measures with their real-photo ceiling.

Three things about this profile are stated rather than hidden. **The transformer is loaded 4-bit or not at all:** 23.8 GB of bfloat16 does not fit a 16 GB GPU, `bitsandbytes` NF4 brings it to about 6.6 GB, and the adapter is trained over — and meant to be served over — that quantised base, whose outputs differ from the bfloat16 checkpoint's by an amount this repository does not measure. **The text encoders (9.7 GB in float16) do not fit beside it:** every prompt must be encoded before the transformer loads, `encode_prompts` refuses to run while the transformer is resident, and the reload step of the tutorial releases the adapted transformer before the fresh one loads. **Generation has no ground truth:** the only assertion with a direction is the one the procedure guarantees — the kept epoch's validation flow-matching loss is no higher than the frozen model's — while the test loss, the CLIP scores and the real-photo ceiling are read as observations, and no human evaluation, FID or preference study exists here.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

###### Primary Intended Uses

Two tasks are exposed. **Few-step text-to-image generation:** input is one or more prompts of 1..1,000 characters (truncated to 256 T5 tokens and 77 CLIP tokens, T5 truncations reported), a seed and a step count (1..8; four by default, the number the model was distilled for); output is one 512 × 512 RGB image per prompt (image *i* seeded with `seed + i`), the pixel mean and the generation record. **Adaptation to captioned images:** given at least four `{id, image, caption}` records (shorter side 256..4,096 px, centre-cropped to 512²), the pipeline fine-tunes the LoRA on the rectified-flow velocity objective over the frozen 4-bit base with a small learning rate and validation-loss epoch selection, generates with the adapted transformer and exports the 380 LoRA tensors as an adapter of about 37 MB. Envisioned application domains are teaching and evaluating parameter-efficient fine-tuning of a large flow-matching generator on a small captioned set — a species, a product, a house style — on a single 16 GB GPU, in research or the DIMER model workbench; prototyping a generation service whose adaptation is then measured on the operator's own held-out images; and producing illustrative images for exploratory work. In a larger system the pipeline is a single-prompt generator and an adaptation baseline; it is not a content-moderated image service, a photo-realistic reference generator, or an image editor.

###### Primary Intended Users

Intended users are machine-learning engineers and researchers who work with latent diffusion or flow-matching models, and educators demonstrating quantised parameter-efficient fine-tuning, in research, teaching or the DIMER model workbench. The pipeline assumes its users understand what a LoRA changes and what it does not (the image-stream attention projections of the transformer; never the VAE, the encoders or the rest of the weights), what 4-bit weight quantisation does to a model's outputs, that a training loss is not an image-quality score and a CLIP similarity is not a human judgement, that six template captions make the sample a teaching example rather than a dataset, and that Apache-2.0 governs the weights while the rights to the images they fine-tune on and generate remain theirs to establish.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** the pipeline generates 512 × 512 images from text in one to eight steps and fine-tunes a LoRA over the 4-bit transformer. It does not run FLUX.1 [dev] or [pro], classifier-free guidance or guidance distillation, ControlNet, inpainting or image-to-image conditioning, DreamBooth identifiers, full or 16-bit fine-tuning, safety filtering of prompts or images, resolutions other than 512², or any human-preference or FID/KID evaluation; and it does not reproduce the published quality results.
2. **Input boundary:** records are RGB images (PIL or file path) with sides within 256..4,096 px and captions of 1..1,000 characters; datasets hold 4..2,000 records with unique ids; prompts are non-empty strings. Other sizes, non-image files, empty captions or duplicate ids are not inputs. Validation is structural: nothing checks that a caption describes its image or that a prompt is one the model can render.
3. **Decision boundary:** not for producing images presented as photographs of real events, people or products, for depicting identifiable people, or for any downstream decision that treats a generated image as evidence. A generated image from this pipeline is an experiment output.
4. **Provenance boundary:** not for settings that require the upstream repository itself as the download source (the contract stages from the mirror and verifies against the committed manifest), that need the bfloat16 transformer served as-is on a 16 GB GPU (it does not fit; the 4-bit load is the contract), or that require the encoders to stay resident beside the transformer; the DIMER upload is on HOLD and the staging and byte-identity state are documented under *DIMER deployment notes* and in `docs/WEIGHTS.md`.

#### Factors

###### Groups

The model generates images of whatever the prompt names, so the relevant groups are the subjects that prompts can depict: people of any age, gender, skin type, body, dress and culture, professions, places, and the animals and objects of the sample. Upstream describes no training data and no per-group evaluation for FLUX.1; this repository's sample prompts name six bird species and no people, and the pipeline measures nothing about how any group is rendered. A deployment that generates depictions of people must measure representation and stereotyping for its prompts itself; the people depicted, and the communities whose likeness the model reproduces or distorts, bear the harm.

###### Instrumentation

The instruments are the training photographs' cameras and, upstream, whatever captioning wrote the training descriptions (undocumented); here, the iNaturalist observers' cameras (phone and DSLR, resized by iNaturalist to the "medium" 500 px variant) and one caption template per species. Photograph resolution, compression and framing reach the model through the VAE as latent detail, and the centre crop discards the edges of every non-square image (reported per record). The 4-bit quantisation is itself an instrument change: NF4 rounds every linear weight of the transformer to one of 16 levels per 64-weight block, and the frozen numbers are read on that model. The measuring instrument for the CLIP scores is `laion/CLIP-ViT-B-32-laion2B-s34B-b79K`, a 224 px image–text model with its own training biases: it rewards images that look like its training distribution and captions phrased like its alt-text, so a rise in a CLIP score is a rise on that instrument, not a rise in quality.

###### Environment

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `diffusers==0.40.0`, `transformers==5.17.0`, `peft==0.21.0`, `bitsandbytes==0.50.2`, `torchao==0.18.0`, `accelerate==1.15.0`, `tokenizers==0.23.2`, `sentencepiece==0.2.2`, `protobuf==7.36.2`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `huggingface-hub==1.32.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0` (`pyproject.toml`; `torchaudio` and `torchao` are never called — they are pinned because `transformers` imports the former when present and `peft`'s LoRA dispatcher refuses an old copy of the latter, both preinstalled on hosted images). The transformer runs 4-bit (NF4, double quantisation) with float16 compute on CUDA and the LoRA parameters in float32 under autocast and loss scaling (`COMPUTE_DTYPE` may be set to `bfloat16` on GPUs that support it natively; the T4 does not); the VAE stays in float32 (its config forces the upcast); the text encoders run in float16 on CUDA and are released before the transformer loads. A CUDA GPU of at least 15 GB with `bitsandbytes` support is required, and about 36 GB of disk for the two snapshots; CPU execution is not supported (4-bit needs CUDA and the transformer would need 48 GB of RAM in float32). The recorded runs are under *Runtime*. Data environment: the model assumes prompts in English resembling captions and renders best the subjects, styles and compositions common in its training corpus; it degrades, without any error, on precise counts, long text rendering, spatial relations, rare subjects and long compositional prompts, and a distilled few-step model trades diversity for speed.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

The pipeline reports three kinds of number and names each. **Held-out flow-matching MSE** (`evaluate`): every photograph is VAE-encoded (seeded posterior sample, shifted and scaled) and packed, mixed with seeded noise at noise levels σ = 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9 as x_σ = (1 − σ)·x₀ + σ·ε, and the transformer's velocity prediction is scored by mean-squared error against the rectified-flow target ε − x₀ — reported as the mean over records and noise levels, per noise level and per record. The seed fixes latents, noise and noise levels, so the frozen and adapted models are compared on identical inputs; this is the training objective on held-out data, not a quality score. **CLIP scores of generated images** (`metrics.score_generations`): `clip_prompt_similarity` (mean cosine between each image and its prompt × 100), `label_accuracy` (the fraction of images whose nearest prompt among the distinct prompts is their own — zero-shot classification with an argmax rule), and `reference_similarity` (mean cosine to the mean embedding of the held-out real photographs with the same caption × 100). **The real-photo ceiling** (`real_photo_baseline`): the same three scores on the held-out photographs themselves, so a reader can see how far a generation sits from a real photograph on this instrument. Every number in this repository is sample-sanity evidence from one seeded run on 12 held-out photographs, 12 validation photographs (epoch selection only) and 6 generated images of the 4-bit model, with no dispersion estimate; no published FLUX.1 result (ELO, human preference, GenEval) is reproduced or claimed.

###### Decision thresholds

The generator has no decision rule; the only rule in the repository is the CLIP `label_accuracy` argmax (a generated image is "correct" when its nearest caption among the dataset's captions is its own), which has no threshold and is labelled as the zero-shot instrument it is. The limits fixed during development are input-contract limits, not decision thresholds — the image-side range, the caption length, the 256-token truncation, the 4..2,000 record range, the 1..8 step range — chosen so that a rejection names a real defect and an acceptance never means "the caption is right" or "the prompt is renderable". An operator who needs a threshold (a minimum CLIP similarity before an image is kept, a maximum loss before an adaptation is accepted) must set it from their own review, and own it. `adapt` selects the epoch with the lowest validation flow-matching MSE, which is the only selection this repository performs.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

Reported metrics come from one seeded run — 12 held-out test photographs for the numbers, 12 validation photographs for epoch selection, one generation per test caption at fixed seeds — with no cross-validation, bootstrap, ensemble, repeated seeds or dispersion: with six captions and six images, any interval would be dominated by which photographs and seeds were drawn. A caller who needs one evaluates many images and seeds across many prompts and reports distributions, and owns that design. Sources of run-to-run variability: the split, the VAE posterior samples, the evaluation noise, the training noise and noise levels and the generation latents are all seeded (`torch.Generator` on the CPU); float16 autocast, the 4-bit dequantisation kernels, non-deterministic GPU attention kernels and the gradient scaler are not controlled, so a re-run can differ in the last decimals of the adapted numbers and in individual pixels of a generated image, which is why the reload-parity check compares a seeded generation by mean absolute pixel difference and the held-out loss by a tolerance rather than byte equality. CLIP similarities are cosine scores on one instrument and are **not calibrated probabilities**; the flow-matching loss is an objective value with no uncertainty estimate beyond the per-record spread the report lists.

###### Mitigations

1. **Supply-chain integrity:** `MODEL_ID`, `MODEL_REVISION`, `STAGING_ID`, `STAGING_REVISION`, `SCORER_ID` and `SCORER_REVISION` are module constants; `verify_snapshot()` checks `modelId`, `revision`, the staging source and the byte size and SHA-256 of every manifest entry (23 files) before any load and raises on the first mismatch (tests flip bytes, change the identity, drop the staging record and delete a file and assert each raises); `verify_scorer_snapshot()` does the same for 9 files. `stage_missing_files()` fetches only manifest-listed files absent on disk, only from the pinned mirror revision, only when `allow_download=True`, and refuses a manifest naming another model; a test asserts the downloader is pointed at `unsloth/FLUX.1-schnell` at `9df3faa7…` and never at the gated upstream. The manifest excludes the mirror's own README and NOTICE and the single-file BFL checkpoints, so nothing the mirror added or relabelled is part of the verified perimeter. `.gitattributes` carries `weights/** -text`.
2. **Safetensors only, no remote code:** every weight file in both snapshots is safetensors; nothing is unpickled anywhere in the pipeline, `trust_remote_code` is never set, and the classes come from `diffusers`, `transformers`, `peft` and `bitsandbytes` on PyPI at exact pins. The transformer is loaded from the verified directory and its parameter count as the checkpoint counts it (11,891,178,560, with packed 4-bit tensors counted at their original shape) and the LoRA tensor count (380) are asserted.
3. **Residency rule:** the text encoders are loaded only by `encode_prompts`, in float16 on CUDA, refused while the transformer is resident, and released by `release_text_encoder` or by `load_transformer`; `generate`, `evaluate` and `adapt` refuse to run on an unencoded prompt rather than reloading the encoders on demand, so an out-of-memory failure cannot be reached by accident; `import_prompt_cache` checks the shape of every entry (256 × 4,096 embeddings and a 768-vector) before adopting it.
4. **Data integrity:** each of the 60 photographs is pinned by byte size and SHA-256 and refused on a mismatch before decoding; `dataset_manifest` records a digest over decoded pixels and captions; `check_split_disjoint` refuses an image present in two splits; the BYOD loader reads only the files `captions.csv` names.
5. **Artifact integrity and scope:** the adapter is safetensors, not a pickle. `load_artifact` validates the manifest **before** deserialising anything: the artifact format and `format_version`, the recorded base model id and revision, the recorded quantisation (`nf4`), exactly one weights entry named `adapter.safetensors` that resolves inside the artifact directory, the LoRA rank / alpha / targets, and a tensor list equal to the exact set of 380 LoRA tensors of this model; the safetensors keys must then equal that set and every shape must match, and the tensors are written in place so the quantised base is never re-serialised. The weights file's SHA-256 is checked against the adjacent manifest — it detects corruption or drift of the file relative to that manifest, not authenticity: an actor who can replace both files can make them agree.
6. **Input integrity:** `evaluate`, `adapt`, `validate_inputs` and `validate_dataset` route through one checker, so their acceptance criteria cannot diverge; it rejects missing keys, unreadable images, sides outside 256..4,096 px, empty or over-long captions, duplicate ids and datasets outside 4..2,000 records; `generate` validates prompts and steps the same way; the centre crop and the token truncation are reported, never silent.
7. **Training discipline:** only the 380 LoRA tensors receive gradients (the 4-bit base, the VAE and the encoders store no graph), the learning rate defaults to 10⁻⁴ and is bounded at 10⁻², gradients are clipped at norm 1, noise levels are drawn uniformly and noise is seeded, gradient checkpointing bounds activation memory, the epoch is selected by validation loss with the frozen model as epoch 0, and `adapt` is transactional — any failure restores the LoRA to its initial values, disables checkpointing, re-freezes the model and clears the adapter.
8. **Import boundary:** manifest verification and input validation run before `torch`, `diffusers`, `transformers`, `peft`, `bitsandbytes`, `safetensors` or `huggingface_hub` are imported (DIMER RTM-001); `tests/test_import_boundary.py` fails if a rejected request reaches a model library, and an AST check fails if any of them is imported at module level.
9. **Honest scope:** validation is labelled structural, the CLIP scores are labelled as instrument readings against a real-photo ceiling, the flow-matching loss is labelled as the objective, every number is labelled as the 4-bit model's, the only assertion with a direction is the guaranteed one (the kept epoch's validation loss is no higher than the frozen model's; the test loss is printed, not asserted), and no upstream quality result is claimed.

Not implemented, and therefore not claimed: no prompt or image safety filter, no watermarking of generated images, no resolutions other than 512², no 16-bit or full fine-tuning, no measurement of what NF4 quantisation costs against the bfloat16 checkpoint, no human or FID evaluation, no calibration of any score, and no measurement beyond the 60-photograph sample.

###### Risks and harms

1. **Fabrication:** a generated image can depict a real person, a real place or a plausible event that never happened, with photographic realism at 512² in four steps; the repository adds no watermark, no provenance metadata and no filter, and Apache-2.0 imposes no use-based restriction, so misuse is bounded only by law and the operator.
2. **Sexual, violent and demeaning content:** the model can render such content and the pipeline does not refuse it; the sample prompts are birds, which proves nothing about other prompts.
3. **Stereotypes and erasure:** the undocumented training corpus carries the biases of its sources; prompts naming professions, nationalities or bodies can be rendered stereotypically, and rare subjects can be rendered as more common ones. Nothing here measures it.
4. **Copyright and likeness:** the training data may include copyrighted works and identifiable people; an operator fine-tuning on photographs they do not own, or generating a living artist's style, carries that exposure.
5. **Quantisation and provenance drift:** an adapter trained over the NF4 base and applied to a bfloat16 or differently quantised copy of the model is applied to a different function; the artifact manifest records `nf4`, and `load_artifact` refuses a manifest recording anything else. The mirror could, in principle, diverge from the upstream in the future; the pinned revision and the committed digests freeze what this repository loads, and the byte-identity evidence is documented in `docs/WEIGHTS.md` rather than assumed.
6. **Overfitting on a few images:** a LoRA trained on 36 photographs of six species learns those photographs; the validation loss guards only against the worst case on the validation images, and the adapted model can lose generality on prompts far from the training captions.
7. **Metric misreading:** a lower flow-matching loss and a higher CLIP score are easy to read as "better images"; neither is, and the card, the README and the notebook say so at every occurrence.
8. **Memory failure modes:** the encoders and the transformer together, or two transformers, exceed a 16 GB GPU; the residency rule makes the first impossible by construction and the tutorial handles the second by releasing before reloading, but a caller who holds a second pipeline object gets an out-of-memory error at load.
9. **Automation bias and proprietary data:** a convincing image invites use without review, and the realistic confidentiality failure is licensed stock or client imagery uploaded to a cloud notebook — inference is local, but the runtime may not be the operator's.

###### Use cases

The model must not be used to generate images that depict identifiable people without their consent, that present a generated image as a photograph of a real event, or that are sexual, violent, harassing or demeaning; it must not be used as evidence about FLUX.1's published quality, which this repository does not measure; it must not be used to redistribute the weights or an adapter without the Apache-2.0 notice; it must not be used to fine-tune on images the operator has no right to use; and it must not be used in any way that breaches the upstream licence or the DIMER deployment terms.

## Input/output contract

- `FluxSchnellPipeline.from_pretrained(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False, use_lora=False, compute_dtype="float16", load_transformer=False)`: stages (when allowed, from the mirror) and verifies the snapshot, loads the VAE (float32), both tokenizers and the scheduler config on a CUDA device (CPU is refused). The transformer and the text encoders are not loaded here.
- `encode_prompts(prompts) -> dict`: loads CLIP-L and T5-XXL on first use (compute dtype), encodes every distinct prompt into the cache (256 × 4,096 embeddings and the 768 pooled vector), reports counts, timings and truncations; refused while the transformer is resident. `release_text_encoder() -> bool`, `export_prompt_cache() -> dict`, `import_prompt_cache(cache) -> int`.
- `load_transformer() -> dict` (releases the encoders first; NF4, parameter count asserted, LoRA attached when `use_lora=True`), `release_transformer() -> bool` (also forgets the adapter state).
- `generate(prompts, *, seed=0, steps=4) -> dict` with `model` (id, revision, key, `adapted`), `steps`, `guidance_scale` (always 0.0), `size`, `scheduler`, `precision`, `images` (per prompt: `prompt`, `seed`, `image` (PIL, 512 × 512 RGB), `pixel_mean`), `seconds`. Every prompt must be encoded first.
- `evaluate(records, *, seed=0, batch_size=2) -> dict`: `metric`, `n_records`, `sigmas`, `seed`, `flow_matching_mse`, `by_sigma`, `per_record`, `adapted`, `seconds`.
- `adapt(train, val=None, *, epochs=4, lr=1e-4, batch_size=1, seed=0, progress=None) -> dict`: bounded AdamW QLoRA fine-tuning (needs `use_lora=True`); returns the LoRA configuration, `trainable_names`, `n_trainable`, `n_total`, `quantization`, `compute_dtype`, hyperparameters, `best_epoch`, per-epoch `history` (epoch 0 = the frozen model, each with `train_loss` and `val_loss`), `n_steps` and `seconds`.
- `save_artifact(output_dir, metadata=None) -> Path` writes `adapter.safetensors` (the 380 LoRA tensors, float32) + `manifest.json` (format, base model id/revision/key/licence, staging source, quantisation, adaptation record, history, tensor names, file size and SHA-256); `from_artifact(..., prompt_cache=None)` / `load_artifact(...)` verify all of that before deserialising.
- `validate_inputs(record) -> dict` (`id`, `size`, `resized_to`, `centre_crop`, `caption_chars`) and `validate_dataset(records, *, min_records=4, max_records=2000) -> dict` (normalised `records`, `n_records`, `n_captions`, `shorter_side`, `centre_cropped`, `resolution`, `digest`, `model_id`) raise exactly what the execution path would raise; `validate_prompts(prompts) -> list[str]`; `preprocess_image(image)` performs the resize and centre crop; `pack_latents` / `unpack_latents` / `latent_image_ids` are the token-layout helpers. `fetch_sample_dataset(cache_dir=None)` / `fetch_corpus` / `read_corpus` / `build_sample_dataset` / `split_dataset` / `load_byod_dataset(path)` / `write_dataset_csv` / `dataset_manifest` / `sample_prompts` / `check_split_disjoint` / `caption_for` are the data helpers; `metrics.ClipScorer(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False)`, `score_generations(scorer, generated, references=None)` and `real_photo_baseline(scorer, records)` are the scoring helpers.
- Constants: `RESOLUTION = 512`, `LATENT_CHANNELS = 16`, `VAE_SCALE = 8`, `PACKED_CHANNELS = 64`, `MAX_PROMPT_TOKENS = 256`, `MAX_CAPTION_CHARS = 1000`, `MIN_IMAGE_SIDE = 256`, `MAX_IMAGE_SIDE = 4096`, `MIN_TRAIN_RECORDS = 4`, `MAX_RECORDS = 2000`, `DEFAULT_STEPS = 4`, `MAX_STEPS = 8`, `GUIDANCE_SCALE = 0.0`, `EVAL_SIGMAS = (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9)`, `QUANTIZATION = "nf4"`, `COMPUTE_DTYPE = "float16"`, `LORA_RANK = 8`, `LORA_ALPHA = 8`, `LORA_TARGETS`, `LORA_MODULES = 190`, `LORA_TENSORS = 380`, `LORA_PARAMETERS = 9338880`, `TRANSFORMER_PARAMETERS = 11891178560`, `TRANSFORMER_TENSORS = 1156`, `ARTIFACT_FORMAT = "org.valcorza.flux-schnell-generation.adapter.v1"`, `MODEL_LICENSE = "apache-2.0"`, `STAGING_ID = "unsloth/FLUX.1-schnell"`.
